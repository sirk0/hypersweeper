Cover the shape exactly with the tiles in the tray. Drag a tile onto the board and it snaps into place; turn and flip the tray to make the rest fit. Any exact cover wins, not only the one the level was cut from, so the puzzle is in seeing which pieces go where before the corners run out.

It starts gently with squares, triangles and hexagons, and goes all the way to tilings that never repeat: Penrose's rhombi and his kites and darts, Ammann–Beenker, the Spectre, and the hat, the first single tile that only tiles the plane aperiodically.

MORE THAN 1,600 LEVELS
• 104 tilings in eleven families: regular, uniform, 2-uniform, Laves, 2-isohedral, pentagonal, isogonal, brick bonds, rep-tiles, spirals and aperiodic
• Every tiling cut into several boards, each a level of its own
• Matching rules and colourings: tiles with lines or coloured marks on their edges that must meet their partners, turning an easy board into a real puzzle
• Rep-tiles: a big copy of a shape to fill with small copies of itself, from the sphinx and the chair to tiles of several pieces moved as one

PLAY YOUR WAY
• Easy, Medium and Hard each deal a random level, with difficulty measured rather than guessed
• Up to three stars a level: finish it, use no extra tiles, and do it without a hint
• Stuck? The hint works out exactly what to do next: a tile to lay, or the tile to take back off
• Pinch to zoom, and drag a tile back to the tray to take it off
• Light and dark themes, and three sound themes
• Haptic feedback on iPhone as tiles land and boards are finished
