Classic minesweeper, but the board can be almost any shape you can imagine: a sphere, a donut, a Möbius strip, a Klein bottle, a Penrose tiling, the hyperbolic plane, or a solid cube of cubes.

The rules you already know: every number tells you how many of its neighbours hide a mine. What changes is the board. A neighbour is any cell that shares a corner, so a hexagon has six neighbours, a triangle has twelve, a square in the hyperbolic plane has twelve too, and a cell inside the volumetric cube has twenty-six. On a torus the board wraps around in both directions; on a Möbius strip, one trip around brings you back mirror-reversed.

MORE THAN 200 BOARDS
• Flat: squares, triangles, hexagons, the uniform and Laves tilings, brick bonds, basket weave and herringbone
• Aperiodic: Penrose rhombi, kites and darts, Ammann–Beenker, the Spectre monotile, and spirals
• Fractals: Sierpiński carpet, pentaflake, Gosper island, sphinx and chair
• Hyperbolic: heptagons, pentagons and squares in the Poincaré disc
• Surfaces: cylinder, torus, Möbius strip, Klein bottle, projective plane, trefoil knot, double torus
• Solids: Goldberg spheres, the Platonic, Archimedean and Catalan solids, framed and stepped polyhedra
• Volumetric: minesweeper in three dimensions, inside a cube of cubes
